## A mechanism is part of a system

My work in FTC and FRC robotics has made me particularly interested in how individual mechanisms affect the behavior of the overall robot. As a mechanical lead, I worked on a shooter pivot redesign involving a 25+ lb assembly and its gearbox. Although the mechanism had a specific task, its design also had to account for the space available in the robot, its weight, and the requirements of the systems around it.

The significance of these constraints becomes clearer when considering how the mechanism would be assembled, wired, and operated. A design that works in isolation can still create problems when it is placed within the rest of the robot. This meant that decisions about the pivot also had to consider what those decisions would require from the electrical and software work.

## Working through the constraints

The redesign involved considering pivot geometry, the power transmission path, and the load across the operating range. These considerations were connected because the torque and gear reduction needed to move the assembly also affected the structure supporting it.

In addition, assembly access and wiring clearance influenced how the mechanism could be arranged. Serviceability was important for the same reason: if a component is difficult to reach, even a small adjustment can require a significant amount of work. These details helped determine whether the design would be practical for the team to use.

## Connecting design and operation

My robotics work also includes Java teleop development. This gave me another way to consider the relationship between what the operator asks the robot to do and how the physical mechanism responds. The mechanical and software sides of the project could be considered separately, but the behavior of the robot still depended on both.

As a mechanical lead, I also had to consider how different people understood these connections. Keeping the requirements of a mechanism clear helped make it possible to coordinate work across the different parts of the robot.

## What I learned

Ultimately, this work made me think more carefully about what it means for a component to be successful. Being able to perform its immediate task is important, but the design also affects how the rest of the robot can be built, operated, and maintained. This relationship between individual decisions and their wider effects is what continues to interest me about engineering.
