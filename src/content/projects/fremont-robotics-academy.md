## Making robotics more accessible

I founded Fremont Robotics Academy to give students a way to explore robotics without participation fees. The program has served more than 100 students across 10 schools, with work that includes teaching, lesson planning, volunteer coordination, fundraising, and school outreach. My interest in this work comes from being able to connect what I have learned through robotics to opportunities for other students to begin learning about it themselves.

## Teaching students who are just starting

Teaching robotics requires me to consider how an idea will be understood by someone who does not have the same background that I do. Although I may be familiar with how a mechanism or a program works, that familiarity does not necessarily make the reasoning behind it clear to a student. An explanation needs to provide enough context for the student to understand what they are trying to do and why a particular approach might work.

Lesson planning gave the program a structure for introducing these ideas. Preparing volunteers and organizing materials were also important because the way an activity was supported affected whether it could be used across different classrooms. These parts of the work made me consider how the learning experience depended on the preparation behind it.

## Organizing the program

In addition to teaching, the program required coordinating with schools, recruiting volunteers, and raising funds. I have led more than 100 volunteers across two countries and 10 annual events, coordinating more than 10,000 volunteer hours since founding. Community outreach and fundraising campaigns have secured $15,000 in grants and donations. Each of these tasks affected whether a lesson could actually take place. The educational and organizational work were therefore closely connected, because an activity needed people, materials, and time in order to become useful to students.

This connection was important to me because it showed how much of making an idea accessible depends on the work surrounding the idea itself. Developing a lesson was one part of the process, while arranging the conditions in which it could be taught was another.

## What this work means to me

Fremont Robotics Academy is an important part of my engineering background because it has made me think about how technical knowledge can be passed on and applied by other people. Being able to build something myself is valuable, but teaching requires me to consider how someone else might develop the understanding needed to build it. I want to continue making that connection between my own learning and opportunities for others to learn.
