## Learning by building

My interest in electrical engineering developed through being able to see how ideas from different fields could be applied to the same project. In competition robotics, mechanical design, electronics, and software each had their own requirements, but the behavior of the robot depended on how they worked together. Although a mechanism could make sense on its own, its usefulness also depended on how it could be assembled, wired, and operated within the rest of the robot. These connections became particularly interesting to me because they made it necessary to consider the wider effects of an individual design decision.

I am pursuing a B.S. in Electrical Engineering at UC San Diego, with a focus on controls and machine learning and expected graduation in 2027. Before transferring, I studied computer science, mathematics, and physics at Ohlone College. This coursework gave me a foundation that I continue to build on through electrical engineering, particularly in hardware design, embedded development, and control systems. Working on projects alongside these subjects gives me a way to connect an explanation of how a system behaves to the actual behavior of that system.

At Triton Robotics, I work as an Electronics Engineer, designing and validating circuits in EasyEDA and developing C++ control algorithms for robot motion and turret rotation. My current work includes a testbench power-module schematic with 24 V distribution and planned 12 V and 5 V outputs.

## My engineering interests

I am particularly interested in the relationship between a physical system and the code used to control it. Microcontrollers, sensors, power distribution, and actuators all have specific functions, but those functions also place requirements on the other parts of a system. For example, a decision about how a mechanism should move affects both the power needed to move it and the way its motion is controlled. Being able to understand these relationships is a large part of what draws me to robotics and embedded systems.

My research interests also include photonics, RF systems, and semiconductor devices. My programming work includes C, C++, Python, Java, and SystemVerilog. In addition, I have explored power and microgrid design, high-power rocketry, and autonomous robotics. Across these areas, I want to develop a better understanding of why a system behaves the way it does and how that understanding can guide changes to its design.

## Leadership and teaching

As a mechanical lead in FTC and FRC robotics, I had to consider how a design would be understood and used by the other people working on the robot. A mechanism involves decisions about its physical behavior, but it also involves coordinating the people responsible for building, wiring, and programming it. This experience made me think more carefully about how clearly a design and its requirements need to be communicated.

I founded Fremont Robotics Academy to offer free, hands-on STEM education, and the program has served more than 100 students across 10 schools. The work has included lesson planning, teaching, recruiting volunteers, fundraising, and coordinating with schools. Although teaching robotics draws on my own technical background, it also requires me to consider how someone encountering these ideas for the first time might understand them. Being able to explain an idea clearly is important because it allows a student to use that explanation when approaching a problem themselves.

My Eagle Scout service project involved restoring emergency supplies at Mission San Jose Elementary School. Rebuilding shelves, replacing expired supplies, and improving labeling and inventory were all parts of the project. The value of this work came from considering how the supplies would be found, used, and maintained by the people who would rely on them afterward.

## Beyond engineering

I enjoy ultimate frisbee, soccer, and cycling. Team sports give me another setting in which individual decisions affect a larger group. Although the circumstances are different from working on an engineering project, communication and an understanding of what other people are doing remain important to how the team functions.

I am also interested in history and public transit. In history, I find it interesting to consider how ideas and decisions from the past continue to affect the present, even when the circumstances around them have changed. Public transit provides a more immediate way to think about these relationships, particularly through how infrastructure affects the places people can reach and the way they move through a city.

## What I want to explore next

I want to continue working on embedded control, sensing, and autonomous robotics. I am interested in research and engineering opportunities where I can develop an idea through implementation and testing while learning from people with different areas of expertise. Ultimately, I want the understanding I gain from these experiences to inform the way I approach the next problem.
